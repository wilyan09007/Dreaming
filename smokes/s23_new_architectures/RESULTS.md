# S23 new architectures (PARTIAL: the pod was cut off, no critic pass)

## In plain words

We asked whether three newer ideas could get the small student (frozen bge-small + logistic regression) to answer on its own, safely, with fewer labelled rows per label than the plain student needs. (1) Semi-supervised probes: use the task's unlabelled stream (messages with no label) to improve the student. (2) PAW, a public "compiler" that turns a task description plus a few examples into LoRA weights for a small language model. (3) TSDAE, a way to adapt the encoder on the unlabelled stream before fitting. Attention-head reweighting was written as a separate script but never ran.

What the partial data says. Two of the probes, the self-training one (TK-KNN-style) and the Sinkhorn balanced-pseudo-label one with a uniform class prior, help clearly at 1 real row per label: accuracy 0.81 -> 0.89-0.91 and certified coverage at the 5% budget 0.13 -> 0.35-0.56 (the four variants are TK-KNN 0.56 / 0.44 and Sinkhorn 0.35 / 0.41, full stream / 24 rows per label). From 3 rows per label on no probe beats the baseline. The label-spreading probes and the Sinkhorn version that estimates the class prior do not help, and LabelSpreading read directly is clearly worse at k=3 (coverage -0.25 +- 0.08). TSDAE adaptation hurts at every k, and hurts more the longer it trains. PAW is far below the plain student at k=1 and zero-shot, and 0.03-0.07 below at k=3 and k=10 (2 cells), but only 7 of its 45 planned fine-tune cells finished (one draw, three of five tasks), so PAW numbers are anecdotes, not estimates. For context, at k=1 S17's LLM-written examples reached 0.91 accuracy and 0.80 coverage (S17 figure, not recomputed here; different draws, not paired), above the best probe here (0.91 / 0.56).

## Setup

Same protocol as S17 (see `../s17_warm_start/RESULTS.md`): 5 new-step tasks (CLINC `travel` and `work`, 15 labels; three 12-label Banking77 sub-tasks), k real rows per label in {1, 3, 10}, 3 draws, calibration = 300 rows, test = official test rows (450 / 450 / 480 / 480 / 479). The "unlabelled stream" for arms 2 and 4 is the task's own train split minus the calibration rows and minus the k labelled rows (1,200-1,415 rows per task, labels hidden); test and calibration rows are never in it.

Metrics: accuracy on the test rows, and certified coverage at a 5% error budget (`common.certify`, fixed grid, Clopper-Pearson, delta 0.05, confidence = top-2 gap, cutoff from the calibration rows applied to the test rows). Every table below is a mean over the cells present in `salvaged_results.json` (tasks x draws); the number of cells is shown (n). Tables are regenerated by `python3 report_salvaged.py`. The JSON stores the realized coverage per cell, not the raw confidences, so the certificate itself was not re-run, only aggregated.

Arms as planned in `run.py`:

1. Baseline LR on k random rows per label; free variant: LR on k x K k-means medoid rows of the unlabelled stream (labels revealed afterwards, i.e. unlimited-oracle selection of which rows to label); ceiling = LR on all pool labels (train minus the 300 calibration rows).
2. Semi-supervised probes on the unlabelled stream, each with the full stream and a random subset of 24 x K stream rows (not stratified by label): TK-KNN-style class-balanced self-training (4 rounds); Sinkhorn balanced pseudo-labels + LR (3 rounds), with a uniform class prior and with an estimated prior; LabelSpreading on a kNN graph, read directly and as pseudo-labels for an LR.
3. PAW public compiler (`programasweights/paw-4b-qwen3-0.6b`, rank-64 LoRA for a frozen Qwen3-0.6B interpreter, letter-answer prompt): zero-shot with a k=0 spec and with a spec holding the k examples, a no-adapter control with the same prompt, then 60 fine-tune steps (lr 5e-4, batch 8) from the compiled adapter versus from a random LoRA init with the same prompt, rows and steps; plus a two-task learning-rate check at k=10.
4. TSDAE (60% word deletion, tied decoder, batch 32, lr 2e-5) on each task's unlabelled train texts, checkpoints at 250 / 500 / 1000 steps (1000 is the planned headline), then encoder frozen + LR on the k rows.
5. Attention-head reweighting (`arm4/run.py`: one learned scalar per attention head vs LoRA on a 0.5B decoder): not part of the `run.py` orchestration that ran, no rows.

## Interpretation

- **Probes on the unlabelled stream: a real but narrow gain at k=1, none from k=3.** TK-KNN (full stream) and Sinkhorn with a uniform prior lift k=1 accuracy by about +0.09-0.10 and certified coverage by +0.22 to +0.43 (the coverage differences are 2.7-4.4 standard errors, with 15 cells that are clustered by task). At k=3 and k=10 no probe beats the baseline. Most are within noise in coverage, but LabelSpreading read directly is clearly worse (full stream: k=3 coverage -0.253 +- 0.076, accuracy -0.036 +- 0.008; 24/label: k=10 coverage -0.133 +- 0.084), and TK-KNN 24/label at k=3 is -0.118 +- 0.107; the other accuracy moves are at most 0.02 (all rows are in the paired table above). The mechanism is visible in the pseudo-label accuracy: at k=1 the first LR is 81% accurate, but its most confident class-balanced picks are 97% correct, and self-training adds many nearly clean extra rows. Label spreading is the weakest family, and the Sinkhorn version that estimates the class prior is worse than the uniform one at k=1 (coverage 0.09 vs 0.35 on the full stream), probably because the estimate is noisy from one row per label. The uniform prior is a good fit here only because these streams are close to class-balanced (CLINC has 100 rows per label); a skewed real stream would need checking, and this was not tested.
- **Probes do not beat LLM-written examples.** For the same five tasks S17 reports 0.91 accuracy and 0.80 coverage at k=1 for the synthetic prior (S17 figure, not recomputed here; different draws, not paired; S17's baseline was 0.80 / 0.14 against 0.81 / 0.13 here), versus 0.91 / 0.56 for the best probe. The two ideas use different inputs and could be stacked, but stacking was not run.
- **k-means medoid selection** (a free way to choose which rows to label, with oracle labels afterwards) gives a small k=1 coverage gain (+0.115 +- 0.089, not distinguishable from zero), nothing at k=3, and +0.043 +- 0.015 at k=10. It is not a reliable lever.
- **TSDAE adaptation hurts.** Accuracy is lower than the plain encoder at every checkpoint and every k; coverage is lower except 250 steps at k=10 (+0.016 +- 0.019, within noise). Both worsen with steps; by 1000 steps accuracy is down 0.16 at k=1 and certified coverage at k=3 is nearly halved (0.34 vs 0.64). The unsupervised reconstruction objective moves the embedding away from the geometry the classes need, and 1000 steps at lr 2e-5 on 1,200-1,400 short texts is probably more than useful. Whether an even shorter run helps (e.g. 100 steps) was not tested; the 250-step checkpoint is already negative.
- **PAW: no evidence of value here.** Zero-shot, the compiled adapter reaches 0.43-0.50 mean accuracy on 12-15 way intent tasks (the same prompt without the adapter gets 0.15), so the compiler does inject real information about the label list. But it is 30-50 points below the plain student at k=1 and 0.03-0.07 below at k=3 and k=10 (2 cells; compiled init 0.93 / 0.83 vs baseline 0.96 / 0.88 at k=10, clinc/travel 0.96 vs 0.99). Adding the examples to the spec is mixed: the means 0.56 / 0.62 / 0.47 at k=1 / 3 / 10 are over different task sets from the 0.50 k=0 mean, and paired against k=0 on the same task the examples raise accuracy in 6 of 8 cells (+0.08 on average; travel +0.10 / +0.38 / +0.07, work +0.30 / +0.18 / +0.10) and lower it only on banking/sub0 (-0.28 / -0.24); the no-adapter control also rises with examples (0.15 -> 0.22-0.26). Draw 0 only, 8 cells, so no conclusion. After 60 fine-tune steps the compiled init is no better than a random LoRA init with the same prompt (0.36 vs 0.35 at k=1; 0.87 vs 0.92 at k=3; 0.93 vs 0.88 at k=10, on 2-3 cells each). The decoder only certifies coverage in 2 of 7 cells (k=10), against 5 of 7 for the baseline. With 7 cells and one draw these numbers can only support "no sign of an advantage", not a ranking of PAW against random init.
- **Attention-head reweighting: untested.**

Recommendation. Do not adopt TSDAE or PAW for the warm start. TK-KNN or uniform-prior Sinkhorn pseudo-labelling is a cheap (mean 0.06-0.26 s per fit, max 0.56 s, CPU) possible add-on at 1 real row per label; it is worth a re-run on a skewed, real stream and stacked on the LLM-written examples before it earns a place, and it adds nothing beyond k=3.

## Caveats

- **Incomplete.** PAW is 7 of 45 fine-tune cells (one draw, three of five tasks, one task with only k=1) and 8 of 45 zero-shot-with-examples cells; the lr check, a second and third draw and attention-head reweighting did not run. There was no critic pass.
- **Disagreements with FINDINGS.md (round 3, S23 bullet), recomputed from the JSON.** (a) Baseline at k=1: FINDINGS says 0.82/0.19, the JSON gives 0.81/0.13 (n=15). (b) "Probes pooled 0.83/0.26": reproduced exactly (0.834/0.255) as the average of all ten probe variants at k=1, so it mixes the helpful TK-KNN / uniform Sinkhorn variants (0.89-0.91 accuracy) with the unhelpful ones and understates the best probes; the two helpful methods (both pools) average 0.90/0.44 (the full-stream pair alone 0.91/0.45). The "no gain from k=3 on" claim holds (probe-average 0.93/0.56 vs baseline 0.92/0.64). (c) TSDAE "0.72/0.06, 0.88/0.41, 0.95/0.84": reproduced only as the average of the three checkpoints (250 + 500 + 1000 steps); the planned headline, 1000 steps, is worse (0.65/0.03, 0.84/0.34, 0.94/0.80). (d) PAW: FINDINGS says "0.36 zero-shot, 0.38 / 0.66 / 0.62 after fine-tuning". The JSON gives 0.50 zero-shot (k=0 spec, letters; 0.43 greedy text); the 0.36 matches the k=1 fine-tuned PAW accuracy, not zero-shot; k=3 and k=10 fine-tune accuracy is 0.87 and 0.93 (n=2 each), not 0.66 and 0.62. Those two figures do appear in the JSON as zero-shot slices: 0.62 is the zero-shot accuracy with a spec holding 3 examples per label, averaged over its 3 tasks (0.625); 0.66 is the same over the two CLINC tasks (0.664); and 0.38 equals the k=0 zero-shot mean over the two CLINC tasks (0.382). The likely explanation is that FINDINGS used zero-shot or partial-snapshot slices labelled as "after fine-tuning". (0.66-0.67 is also the mean over all 7 fine-tune rows and over all 7 random-init rows.) The qualitative conclusion, a clear gap to the baseline at k=1, the figures in FINDINGS should be replaced by this file's.
- Five tasks, 3 draws, 15 cells per probe cell; differences in certified coverage below about 0.1 are within noise, and the k=1 coverage figures are jumpy: the baseline certifies 0.00 on three of five tasks. Standard errors treat the 15 cells as independent although they share tasks.
- The unlabelled stream is the task's own train split (about 80-100 rows per label for CLINC, similar for Banking77), a clean, class-balanced stand-in for production traffic. Real streams may be skewed or contain out-of-scope rows, which would hurt the uniform-prior Sinkhorn and TK-KNN's class-balanced selection.
- The probes were evaluated with default hyper-parameters (4 TK-KNN rounds, 3 Sinkhorn rounds, 24/label subset size); none were tuned, and ten variants were compared on the same cells, so the best of them is mildly optimistic.
- LabelSpreading hit a divide-by-zero warning in sklearn (rows unreached by the graph); those rows are handled in the pipeline's code as unreached, but I did not audit how that affects the "direct" variant's low accuracy.
- Realized error: 2 of 694 certified cells were above the 5% budget (LabelSpreading-direct full 6.5%; TSDAE 1000 steps 10.0%, about 219 answered rows); both are clinc/travel, k=1, draw 0. With delta = 0.05 per cell a few such cells are expected, and single cells have only 450-480 test rows.
- PAW comparison uses the pod's letters-prompt formulation, not label-name generation; 60 fine-tune steps at one learning rate (5e-4) on 1-10 rows per label; the lr check did not run. A different recipe could change the compiled-vs-random comparison.
- Numbers for S17 (synthetic prior) quoted for context are copied from that file, not recomputed.
