# Literature check: do confidence heads and hypernetworks earn a place?

Date 2026-10-17. WebSearch/WebFetch only, no code. Most papers were read at abstract or landing-page level (plus the full HTML of Xiong 2024, LM-Polygraph, Text-to-LoRA); numbers quoted below come from those reads. "Dreaming" = missions.md; S-numbers = smokes in FINDINGS.md.

## Part A. Is a second model that predicts "is the first model right?" useless because it is all in the logits?

### Summary
- **For a well-trained typed classifier, the literature says the model's own softmax score is a very hard baseline to beat.** Feng et al. report that the gains of SelectiveNet and Deep Gamblers come from a better classifier, not from their learned selection head, and that plain Softmax Response beats them. Zhu et al. say the same for failure prediction (max class probability beats many fancier methods), and Desai and Durrett find fine-tuned BERT/RoBERTa are well calibrated in-domain. This matches S1 (top-2 gap equals max-prob) and S3 (a learned head helps only modestly).
- **An external head demonstrably helps in four situations.** (1) Distribution shift: a trained calibrator reached 56.1% coverage at 80% accuracy versus 48.2% for MaxProb (Kamath 2020). (2) Label noise, specialist downstream models and train/test shift, which Jitkrittum et al. identify as exactly where confidence thresholding is suboptimal. (3) Extra inputs the logits do not contain: LM-cascade deferral rules that add token-level uncertainty and embeddings beat simple max-prob (Gupta 2024), and a head trained on human corrections sees reviewer behaviour no logit can encode (Mission 4, S3). (4) Closed models with no logprobs.
- **For frontier-LLM (teacher-style) calls there are no usable logits, and the ranking of signals is stable.** Vanilla verbalized confidence is overconfident and near chance at ranking errors (Xiong: average AUROC 51-63), though it is better calibrated than the token probabilities of RLHF models (Tian 2023). Sampling plus consistency is the best black-box signal (Xiong: ~73 average AUROC, GSM8K with GPT-3.5 from 55 to 93). Semantic entropy extends this to free text (Kuhn 2023, Farquhar 2024) but misses consistent mistakes. A surrogate open model's logits scored a black-box model's answers better than its verbalized confidence on 9 of 12 datasets (Shrivastava 2023).
- **Output length decides whether logits suffice.** LM-Polygraph (TACL 2025): on 1-2 token / multiple-choice outputs the information-based scores (MSP, perplexity) are the best or near best and sampling-diversity methods lag; on long generations the order flips and MSP falls to roughly random. Dreaming's typed steps are the short-output case; its free-text steps are the long-output case.
- **Hidden-state probes beat logits and verbalized confidence in-distribution but are fragile and need weights.** Semantic Entropy Probes, question-only probes and truthfulness probes all report gains over black-box signals, yet they fail to generalize across datasets (Orgad 2024) and a 2,000-probe study finds poor robustness beyond near-OOD (April 2026). Dreaming hosts its students, so hidden states are available, but S3 already saw an embedding-feature head lose to a score-only head at ~1,000 corrections.
- **Cascade routers split the same way.** Trained scorers (FrugalGPT's DistilBERT) work but retrain when models change; answer-agreement cascades need no training and no logprobs (Yue 2023, Kolawole 2024, Soiffer 2025, DART 2026). Prompt-only routers plateau and kNN matches them (Li 2025, Lu 2026), which agrees with S5; the plateau paper's suggested fix is a signal from the small model's own output.

## Part B. Can a hypernetwork still earn a place as a meta-learner or transfer learner for few-shot classification?

### Summary
- **Hypernetwork few-shot methods exist and are competitive on image benchmarks, but their reported edge is over other meta-learners, not over a linear head on strong features.** HyperTransformer generates CNN weights from the support set and is best for small target networks; for larger targets, generating only the last layer is competitive. HyperMAML "consistently outperforms MAML"; HyperShot, CNAPs (an adaptation network modulating the classifier) and Zhao 2020 are the same family. Zhao 2020 finds parity with MAML variants when the target is over-parametrized and a win only in the compressive regime.
- **Simple baselines match or beat meta-learners once the features are good.** Chen 2019: a baseline with standard fine-tuning is competitive and compares favourably under domain shift. Tian 2020: a linear classifier on a frozen supervised or self-supervised embedding beats the meta-learners tried. Hu 2022: pretrain, lightly meta-train, fine-tune beats sophisticated meta-learners on Meta-Dataset. A 2024 molecular study reports linear/quadratic probes competing with meta-learning and being more robust to label shift.
- **On text the picture is the same, with one counterexample.** SetFit (contrastively fine-tuned sentence encoder plus logistic head) with 8 examples per class is competitive with RoBERTa-large on 3k, and direct fine-tuning of a pretrained LM is strong for few-shot intent detection from 2 examples per class. LEOPARD is the counterexample: BERT-based meta-training beat pretraining plus fine-tuning by 14.6% relative at 4 examples per label on 17 tasks.
- **LLM-scale weight generators work but need many tasks and give small margins.** Text-to-LoRA (ICML 2025) trained on 479 tasks reaches 67.7 vs 66.3 for a multi-task LoRA on 10 unseen benchmarks (+1.4), and 73.9 vs 71.9 on the 8 non-code ones; with 64 training tasks it is 66.0, at or below the multi-task baseline, and the small generator (4.9M) did not gain from more tasks. Drag-and-Drop LLMs, HyperTuning and Generative Adapter are the same idea; their abstracts claim gains but give no head-to-head against a plain head.
- **No 2024-2026 paper found that tests a hypernetwork classifier against a prototype or logistic-regression head on modern frozen foundation features**, and no recent dedicated survey of hypernetworks for few-shot classification; the question is open in the literature, but the 2019-2024 baseline papers all point one way.

### When does a hypernetwork win (synthesis from the sources above)
1. The target model is small or compressive and a fixed task-independent embedding is not enough (HyperTransformer, Zhao 2020). Theory (Galanti, Wolf 2020) says a hypernetwork can need orders of magnitude fewer trainable parameters than an embedding-conditioned network for structured targets.
2. There are many training tasks, hundreds not tens (Text-to-LoRA: 479; even then the gain over multi-task training is ~1-2 points), and training needs careful initialization (Beck 2022).
3. There are no strong frozen features, so the generator must produce the features' adaptation too, or per-task gradient steps are unaffordable (no-backprop adaptation: HyperTuning, HyperFast for tabular data).
4. Domain shift between tasks is small; under shift, fine-tuned simple baselines hold up better (Chen 2019, 2024 molecular probes).

**Do these apply to Dreaming's students?** Mostly no. The student is a frozen or lightly fine-tuned bge-small encoder with a linear head; the features are strong and the head is the only task-specific part, which is exactly the regime where prototypes and logistic regression match meta-learners. The task count is tens of call sites per customer, not hundreds (S2: 7 training domains, 105 labels, lost to label-embedding baselines; the literature's working point is 64-479 tasks). Condition 3 (instant student with no backprop and no traces) is real but is already covered more cheaply by LLM-written examples (S2b: $0.005, 73-87% day zero) and by a shared workflow-pretrained encoder (S8 phase B, the cheap form of what LEOPARD does). Revisit a generator only if Dreaming accumulates hundreds of call sites across customers, and then compare it to the shared-encoder baseline first.
