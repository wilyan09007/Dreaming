# Orthogonal methods to what Dreaming already tried (research, 2026-10-17)

Scope: 2025-26 methods with a genuinely different mechanism from FINDINGS.md (students on frozen/fine-tuned/LoRA; grid Clopper-Pearson; hypernets and LLM-written examples; TF-IDF/HGB tool predictors; prompt-embedding routers; leave-one-out pruners; record-replay; batch-1 pinning). Method: WebSearch plus WebFetch of arXiv abstract pages, GitHub READMEs and the Anthropic pricing page. "Fetched" = I read the primary page; "snippet" = search-result summary only (see Unverified). Smoke budgets: CPU or one RTX 4090 at $0.34/h, under 1 h.
Honest headline: for problems 1, 3, 5, 6 the strongest new options change the *guarantee or the arithmetic*, not the predictor. For problem 2 no paper predicts "cheap agrees with expensive" from hidden states (only "cheap is correct"), so that test would be new. For problem 4 pure zero-shot of every model I found plateaus near what S2b already reaches.

## Problem 1. Certifying error against a noisy reference
**1a. Design-based anytime-valid audit (betting / e-process on the audit stream).**
- Names: Active, anytime-valid RCPS (Xu, Karampatziakis, Mineiro; NeurIPS 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10490 ; betting confidence sequences (Waudby-Smith & Ramdas, JRSSB 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.09686 ; Anytime-Valid Conformal Risk Control (Hultberg et al., 2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04364 .
- Mechanism: a wealth process tests every candidate error level; labels are queried by a randomized policy under a label budget, so the guarantee holds at all times. 2602.04364 gives quantile-based anytime guarantees with a matching lower bound.
- Result: 2406.10490 reports higher utility with fewer labels than naive labelling baselines (no numbers on the abstract page). 2602.04364 reports simulations and real examples, no numbers on the page.
- Code: none found for either paper; `confseq` (pypi, early-stage, already noted in certification-deferral.md) implements the betting CS.
- My inference, not a claim of these papers: if audit coins have known probability pi_t, the IPW increments are a martingale conditional on the past whatever the clustering of the data, so the sequence bounds the *realized* error of served traffic. It stops being a bound on future population risk; say so in the product.
- Related ablation on our exact datasets: Basu, "Cross-Domain UQ for Selective Prediction: Bound Ablation with Transfer-Informed Betting" https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08907 (Mar 2026, fetched, no code): nine bound families x union bound vs Learn-then-Test fixed-sequence; LTT drops the ln(K) union penalty (MASSIVE, alpha=0.10: 94.0% guaranteed coverage vs 73.8% Hoeffding); on a tiny n=280 set its Transfer-Informed Betting reaches 18.5% coverage at alpha=0.10, 5.4x LTT+Hoeffding; Banking77 (n=13K) and CLINC150 included. Caveat from another paper (ICU-alarm certification, https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12365, snippet): fixed-sequence loses power with Clopper-Pearson because its acceptance region is not a prefix of the ordering.
- Smoke (CPU): replay S4's 6 worlds as a 10k-call stream, 5% audit rate; compare (i) grid CP on the calibration slice, (ii) IPW WSR confidence sequence on the audit rows, (iii) LTT fixed-sequence on S1's calibration slices. Metrics: coverage of the true realized error, calls until a 1% / 5% certificate, audit labels used. Feasible, $0.

**1b. Conformal Selective Acting (CSA).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20270 (2026, fetched). One Ville-type e-process per threshold on a Bonferroni grid with predictable bets; deploy the largest certified threshold; selective-risk bound alpha + O(N^-1/2), rate-optimal certification time. Claims to be the only one of ten methods valid and non-refusing on all cells: 8 specialist benchmarks (480 streams), 16 adversarial shift cells, 5 live online-LoRA cells (10,300 rounds, 4 base models). No code. Why orthogonal: one certificate that survives model updates (our retrain loop) and controls error among *answered* calls. Smoke (CPU): implement the per-threshold e-process on S1/S3's confidence grid, compare certified coverage with grid CP at n=200/500/1000 and across the 770 -> 3080 -> 9993 retrain sequence. Theory assumes isotonic-calibrated monotone risk; check.

**1c. Hierarchical group-conditional CRC (HG-CRC).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24562 (2026, fetched). Per-node thresholds over a user-defined group hierarchy, Bonferroni across nodes, leaf-first with fallback to coarser nodes. Standard CRC violated the budget in up to 47% of trials under mild group-composition shift (ARC-Challenge, MMLU-Pro; Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-3-4B); HG-CRC 0% empirical violations at a 22-37 point participation cost; dropping the difficulty level returns violations to ~11%. A random-label-flip experiment exists (per search snippet: abstains more, participation falls sharply at 10% flips). No code. Smoke (CPU, S4 data): groups = merchant cluster / novelty bin / predicted label; compare violation rate and coverage with the global grid.

**1d. Noise-aware certification via replicates or PPI.** "Noisy but Valid" (ICLR 2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20913 (judge TPR/FPR from a small human set, variance-corrected threshold, finite-sample Type-I control; no code); "How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations" https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21140 (Rogan-Gladen correction, adaptive calibration allocation); "Efficient Inference for Noisy LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation" (ICML 2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05420 (PPI vs RG; RG intervals extremely wide on their real data); GLIDE library https://github.com/EmertonData/glide (PPI++, stratified, active and cost-optimal samplers; permissive license). Einbinder et al. (JMLR 2024) https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14295 : conformal stays conservative under dispersive label noise. Mechanism: treat each teacher draw as a noisy measurement of a consensus label, estimate flip rates from replicates, certify against the consensus; or let a cheap judge label the audit pool and debias with a few teacher labels. Smoke ($0, existing S13 data): student error vs draw 1, draw 2, majority-of-5; test whether a flip-corrected bound certifies budgets below the 3-15% floors. Also WATCH (weighted-conformal martingales for drift alarms, ICML 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04608 : no code seen.

## Problem 2. Per-call "would the cheap config agree?"
**2a. PIKA / "LLMs Encode Their Failures".** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09924 (2026, fetched) ; code https://github.com/KabakaWilliam/llms_know_difficulty (MIT; needs conda + CUDA; pretrained probes at HF CoffeeGitta/pika-probes). Linear probe on the end-of-input hidden state before any generation; utility router and cascade router notebooks. Result: routing across a model pool beats the best single model with up to 70% cost reduction on MATH (README claim). Probes predict the model's own success, so relabel as "cheap output equals expensive output". Weakness: math/code correctness, not typed-step agreement with a noisy teacher.
**2b. "LLM Router: Rethinking Routing with Prefill Activations".** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.20895 (2026, fetched). Encoder-Target Decoupling: an open-weight model's residual stream predicts a *different*, closed model's correctness; SharedTrunkNet MLP over several models. LLMRouterBench: mean AUC 0.856 vs 0.804 (best semantic baseline) on the Frontier pool; +10.9 pp accuracy and 74.3% savings (Frontier), +4.2 pp / 64.2% (Small), +1.2 pp / 29.3% (Mixed). Best encoders were 35B-122B (Qwen3.5, GPT-OSS); no small-encoder numbers seen; no code link seen.
**Smoke (4090, ~$0.10, <1 h):** Qwen3-1.7B (or Qwen3-8B 4-bit) last-token states at 3 layers over the 36.5k RouterBench prompts (~10-15 min), logistic probe for "cheap >= expensive", AUC vs the prompt-embedding router (0.63-0.69) and cost saving at <=1 point loss vs the random-mix line (S5 revision). Pass bar: clear AUC gain AND beating random mixing. Only then spend ~$3-4 on 2k paired Banking77 labels (nano vs E1) to test the typed-step case.
**2c. LatentGate (ACL 2026 industry; abstract only).** https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-industry.153/ : mean-pooled hidden states of a frozen small LM, PCA-whitening against anisotropy, linear probe; 98.8% in-domain / 80.0% OOD over 100 agents, 92.9% on CLINC150, ~28 ms on a T4; 13-22 points over embedding baselines. Not an agreement signal, but a free trick for S1: add PCA-whitening before the LR head (5 min CPU).
Already planned, so not counted as new: semantic-agreement cascades https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21837 (agreement among sampled small-model outputs; 40% of large-model budget, up to 60% lower latency) = FINDINGS open item 2. Speculative cascades (ICLR 2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19261 needs both models' token distributions, so it does not fit API tiers.

## Problem 3. Tool-return and next-call prediction
Covered in tool-prediction-speculation.md (PASTE, AOSpec, TVCache, MirrorAPI, GTM, Qwen-AgentWorld, DreamGym); not repeated. New mechanisms:
**3a. TraceCompiler.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02680 (Aug 2026, fetched; paper CC BY 4.0, no code found). Adds an inter-tool edge only when a consumer argument value is uniquely traceable to an earlier output; bindings classified as constant, user input, copied output, transform or residual LLM decision. Def-use rule: precision 0.928, recall 0.943 over 15,775 edges (adjacency F1 0.711); AppWorld 0.993 precision on 563 token edges (a self-consistency check); Venmo money-request intent 34 -> 11 API calls, 15/21 leave-one-out folds pass; declines to compile when an irreversible side effect is under-determined. Orthogonal because it predicts the *call arguments* by dataflow instead of the return. Smoke (CPU, $0): on tau-bench/tau2 trajectories extract exact-string provenance edges; report the share of calls whose arguments are fully instantiable from earlier returns (ceiling for speculation; Speculative Actions got 22-38%).
**3b. Automata from Agent Traces.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23670 (ICML 2026 workshop, fetched; no code). Merge a trace corpus into one FSM (7-43 states, held-out fitness >= 0.997, built in milliseconds); FSM-state context beats Agent Workflow Memory next-step prediction on every ground-truth-matched dataset (no accuracy numbers on the page); failure AUROC up to 0.94 from partial traces. Smoke (CPU): k-tails/state-merge on tau-bench; next-tool top-1/top-3 vs HGB and n-gram; failure AUROC as an early-stop for mission 7 rollouts.
**3c. Agentic Plan Caching.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14852 (NeurIPS 2025, search listings only): keyword -> plan-template cache, small planner adapts the template; 50.31% lower cost, 27.28% lower latency, ~96.6% of full-planner performance (v1 said 46.62%). Caches plans, not tool returns; no code found (the MLSys26 AgenticCache repo is a different project). Only relevant for planner steps; no smoke.

## Problem 4. Day-zero / few-row students
**4a. TabICLv2.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.11139 (ICML 2026) ; https://github.com/soda-inria/tabicl (BSD-3, `pip install tabicl`, ckpt tabicl-classifier-v2-20260212, `support_many_classes=True`, pretraining code open; H100 predicts 50k x 100 in <10 s; CPU supported). v1 handled >10 classes by hierarchical decomposition (v2's many-class procedure not confirmed). Mechanism: a transformer pretrained on a large synthetic task prior; the classifier is "fit" in one forward pass over the labelled rows. This differs from our failed hypernetwork because the prior covers millions of tasks, not 7 domains. Risks: no text-embedding example in the repo; 384-d x 77 classes is outside what I could find evaluated; LR on good embeddings may simply tie. TabPFN-2.5 is limited to 10 classes natively and its weights are non-commercial (hypernetwork.md). Related: "Towards Pretraining Text Encoders for TabPFN" https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04876 (ICML 2026, mixed results).
Smoke (CPU/4090, $0, ~20 min): cached bge-small embeddings from S1; Banking77 at 1/3/10 rows per label, with and without the S2b synthetic examples in context; compare LR, prior-regularised LR, TabICLv2; metric = accuracy and rows to a 5% certificate. Expect a gain only below ~5 rows per label.
**4b. BTZSC zero-shot benchmark (ICLR 2026).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11991 ; https://github.com/IliasAarab/btzsc (Banking77 is one of 22 datasets). Qwen3-Reranker-8B macro-F1 0.72 (intent 0.70), gte-large-en-v1.5 0.62 (intent 0.59), Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B 0.61 (intent 0.55), Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B 0.58 (intent 0.56), best NLI cross-encoder 0.60 (intent 0.45). Reading: label-description zero-shot plateaus at 55-70%, matching day-zero-student.md; this confirms the plateau and does not beat S2b's 72.6%. No smoke.
**4c. GLiNER2 (205M encoder, CPU-friendly).** https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.18546 ; https://github.com/fastino-ai/GLiNER2 . A search summary says zero-shot Banking77 0.70 vs DeBERTa-v3 0.42, but the abstract page does not confirm it and day-zero-student.md measured GLiClass/NLI near 56%. 10-minute CPU test on 1000 Banking77 rows; if ~0.70 it is an ensemble member for the day-zero prior.
Gaps: I found no 2025-26 test-time-training or semi-supervised method for text classification with code that fits (TTT work found is on abstract reasoning, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07279 ; TK-KNN pseudo-labelling is 2022). Putting all 77 labels in a small LLM prompt collapses (4.0 macro-F1 for Mistral-7B, https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12332), so in-context LLM classification is a dead end without retrieval of demonstrations (2023 method, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10954).

## Problem 5. Fewer input tokens, same output
**5a. Caching and Batch economics (makes pruning conditional).** Source fetched 2026-10-17: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing . Cache write 1.25x (5 min) or 2x (1 h) base; cache hit 0.1x base, 0.05x on Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, 0.025x on Fable 5.1. Sonnet 5.5: $2 in / $10 out, hit $0.10; Haiku 5.5: $0.10 / $0.50, hit $0.01; Batch is 50% of both (Sonnet 5.5 $1 / $5) and "multipliers stack". Consequences: (i) pruning stable prefix tokens saves at most 5-10% and breaks the cache; pruning only pays on per-call variable tokens (S6b's paragraphs, histories); (ii) output tokens cost 5x input, so a thinking step is governed by thinking budget, not input size; (iii) shadow/audit teacher calls are not latency critical, so Batch halves the binding cost for free. Not checked: OpenAI/Google/OpenRouter cache rules for the models the smokes used. Smoke ($0, 20 min): per call site, stable vs variable token split from the S5/S6/S13 logs, then $ per call under no-cache / cache / cache+prune / batch.
**5b. ACON.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00615 (ICML 2026, PMLR 306) ; https://github.com/microsoft/acon (MIT). Gradient-free: an LLM reads cases where the full context succeeds but the compressed one fails and rewrites the compression *guideline*; the optimized compressor is distilled to a small model (>95% accuracy kept, abstract). AppWorld / OfficeBench / multi-objective QA: peak tokens -26-54% with accuracy kept or improved. Different from a span scorer: LLM rewriting guided by failure analysis, works on API models, our ablation labels are exactly its failure signal. Distilled weights not stated as released. Smoke (~$1-2, ~1 h): S6b HotpotQA typed step, 3 guideline rounds with haiku-5.5 on 100 train questions, test on 100, tokens vs agreement against the learned scorer's 82% / 92.5%.
**5c. Observation masking.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433 (NeurIPS 2025 DL4C workshop; code and data released). Replace old tool observations with a placeholder: about half the cost of a raw agent and the same SWE-bench Verified solve rate as LLM summarization across 5 model configs; hybrid -7% vs masking. A near-free baseline any learned pruner must beat on multi-turn call sites. CPU smoke on tau-bench traces.
**5d. Provence** (ICLR 2025; https://huggingface.co/naver/provence-reranker-debertav3-v1 ; code in github.com/naver/bergen): reranker with a per-token keep head, "up to 80% compression" per a third-party summary. A variant of the cross-encoder baseline we already ran; low orthogonality. Not found: structured-input compression with guarantees; learned in-model pruning is provider-side for hosted models, so not testable by us.

## Problem 6. Byte-exact replay
Covered in determinism.md (VLLM_BATCH_INVARIANT, SGLang, LLM-42, cross-SKU GEMM, same-SKU audit 2606.00279); not repeated. New or orthogonal:
**6a. TOPLOC.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16007 (ICML 2025) ; https://github.com/PrimeIntellect-ai/toploc (MIT, `pip install toploc`, 62 stars). Hash of top-k activations as a polynomial congruence, tolerant to reordering/hardware noise; 258 bytes per 32 new tokens; paper reports 100% detection of model, prompt and precision changes. Replaces "same bytes" by "verifiably same model and input within tolerance". Built for decoder LLM activations; encoders untested. Smoke (CPU, ~20 min): bge-small embeddings from S7's 8 byte-distinct runs (batch composition, 1/2/4/8 threads): do all proofs verify, and does fp16/int8 or a swapped checkpoint fail?
**6b. Integer path.** "Power-of-Two INT8 Quantization Scales" https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00363 (Sept 2026, fetched, no code): requantize weight scales to powers of two so CUTLASS and Triton agree bitwise at every linear layer (196/196 and 252/252 vs 8/196 and 10/252); byte-identical generations at 1.7B/8B/14B on 8/8 prompts; perplexity +0.32% / -0.28% / +0.48%; no cross-GPU or batch claim. "Is INT8 Portable?" https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16085 (code https://github.com/yyshin-katech/embedded-ai-quantization-guide/tree/paper1-v1): INT8 predictions matched 1000/1000 when targets share an integer kernel, 958-965/1000 otherwise; FP32 was bit-identical across all pairs; divergence sits in the rescale after exact int32 accumulation. Smoke (CPU, ~15 min): ORT dynamic-int8 bge-small, count distinct output bytes across threads {1,2,4,8} and batch compositions vs the fp32 figure (8 distinct). Expect partial (attention softmax and LayerNorm stay float).
**6c. Weights attestation.** OpenSSF `model-signing` v1.0 (Sigstore; with NVIDIA and HiddenLayer) https://blog.sigstore.dev/model-transparency-v1.0 : signs the hash of every model file with a transparency log. A third-party-verifiable version of our "weights hash". Not a determinism method. I found no deterministic/batch-invariant mode for TensorRT-LLM and no GPU-attestation material.

## Problem 7. Orthogonal to the premise, and industrial analogues
**7a. Auto: The AGI Compiler** https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04542 (Jul 2026) ; https://github.com/RightNow-AI/auto (Apache-2.0, 129 stars, Rust 1.96.1 + flatc 25.12.19, 3 commits). Records frontier-agent spans, finds deterministic ones (488/560 = 87.1%), compiles to WASM artifacts plus distilled models; guard = trigram-Jaccard distance with split-conformal calibration; novel input deopts to the frontier model and the trace recompiles. 300-item stream with three shifts: 59 -> 2 micro-dollars per item (6.4x), 96.9% parity on witnessed inputs (124/128); a deliberately loose guard returned 48.9% silently wrong answers. Self-reported, single benchmark. Closest thing to Dreaming's loop that I found. Smoke (CPU, ~1 h): run `bash evals/toy-agent/e2e.sh` (paid paths off by default) and copy the loose-guard test onto S4's vendor_lookup: lexical conformal guard vs our TF-IDF + CP certificate on novel-merchant false skips (we saw 0.5% calibrated, 4.2% uncalibrated).
**7b. Anthropic advisor tool** https://claude.com/blog/the-advisor-strategy (beta header `advisor-tool-2026-03-01`, tool `advisor_20260301`, `max_uses` cap). The cheap executor decides mid-run when to call a stronger advisor inside one request. Sonnet + Opus advisor: +2.7 pp SWE-bench Multilingual and -11.9% cost per task; Haiku + Opus: BrowseComp 41.2% vs 19.7% alone, 85% cheaper than Sonnet solo at 29% lower score (vendor-reported). Self-escalation instead of pre-call prediction; relevant for free-text steps no student can take. Smoke (~$2-5): draft_note, Haiku 5.5 alone vs with advisor vs Sonnet, LLM judge validated by a second judge.
**7c. Production routers.** LiteLLM Auto Router https://docs.litellm.ai/blog/auto-router-production-savings : 272,876 requests / 7.08B tokens (2026-04-15 to 08-09), $11,736 spent vs $23,985 at top tier (51.1%), ~95% of requests not needing the flagship, four-tier complexity classifier tuned by hand; no quality measurement (counterfactual re-pricing only). OpenAI GPT-5 router (system card, via snippet): trained on manual model switches, preference ratings and measured correctness. Takeaway: hand-set per-tier mapping captured half the cost, consistent with S5's "per-site, not per-input"; none of these carry a certificate.
**7d. Free 2x on the binding cost:** run shadow/audit teacher calls through the Batch API (50%, stacks with cache hits). See 5a.
**7e. Amortization papers.** Agentic Compilation (compile-once JSON workflow for web automation; 80-94% zero-shot compile success, $0.002-0.092 per compile; single author) https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09718 ; SKILL-DISCO https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26669 ; SpeedRunner https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.11338 ; Reason Wide, Not Deep https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.07885 . In these searches none learns a per-step skip decision with an error certificate; Auto (7a) is the only one with a guard.
